Perception

Making sense of the senses — the process of interpreting sensory information to guide action.

📖What it means

Perception is the process of interpreting sensory information to make decisions about appropriate responses. It is the second stage of information processing (after input), transforming raw sensory data into a meaningful understanding of the environment.

Skilled performers perceive situations more efficiently than novices: they are better at selectively attending to relevant cues, recognising patterns and predicting what will happen next (anticipation). This allows quicker, more accurate decision-making.

Perception is influenced by arousal and attention: at optimal arousal, relevant cues are attended to; at very high arousal, attentional narrowing (cue utilisation) can cause relevant cues to be missed. Improving perceptual skills is an important part of developing expertise.

🏅Sporting examples

Pattern recognition

An experienced footballer perceives patterns in play quickly, reading the game faster than a novice.

Anticipation

A skilled cricketer's perception of the bowler's action helps them anticipate the delivery early.

Arousal effect

Over-arousal narrows attention so that relevant perceptual cues are missed.

✍️Using the term in context

Define perception in the information processing model:

"Perception is the process of interpreting sensory information to understand a situation and guide decisions; skilled performers perceive more efficiently through pattern recognition and anticipation, and arousal affects which cues are attended to."

Place it in the input → perception → decision → output model.

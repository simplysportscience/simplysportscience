Unit 7 60 GLH Pearson Set Assignment

Careers in Sport

Students will be introduced to different career opportunities in the global sports sector and the skills required to succeed in them. They will develop employability and digital skills, while exploring how to prepare for, and access, a wide range of sport-related roles and progress within them.

1

Overview

Sport offers a wide range of careers, from coaching and fitness to sports science, development and leisure management. This unit helps students understand those careers, how people progress through them, and the professional standards that employers expect, including safeguarding and health and safety.

Students also learn how recruitment works in practice. They analyse a job role, audit their own skills, apply for and interview for that role, and build a personal career plan. The unit is assessed through a Pearson Set Assignment.

Learning aims explained

Learning aim A: Investigate careers and pathways in the sports industry

Students explore the range of roles in sport, including coaching, teaching, fitness, sports development, sports science and leisure management. They study routes into those roles, from entry-level jobs to qualifications and continuing professional development, and the employment types on offer. They also cover safeguarding, health and safety, and professional standards, which apply across all of these careers.

Strong responses Compares two pathways on entry requirements, progression and employment type, and explains how safeguarding or professional standards affect the role.

Learning aim B: Explore recruitment processes for a selected job role in sport

Students analyse a job description and person specification to understand the duties, qualifications and skills a role needs. They complete a personal skills audit, then apply for and attend an interview for the chosen role. They reflect on how well the recruitment process worked and how ready they are for the role.

Strong responses Matches their own evidence directly to the person specification, and reflects honestly on gaps and how to close them.

Learning aim C: Develop and review a personal career plan for progression in sport

Students write a personal career plan with short-, medium- and long-term goals. They explain how each goal supports progression towards their chosen career, and evaluate which planning strategies are most likely to support long-term progression.

Strong responses Sets goals that are specific and dated, and explains why each step moves the student closer to the chosen role.

6

Worked examples

Sample responses show the standard expected at each level. They are examples, not model answers to copy.

PassA.P1

Explain different careers and progression pathways in the sports industry.

A sports coach can start at grassroots level, for example volunteering at a local club, and progress to a paid role with a national governing body after gaining a recognised coaching qualification. A fitness instructor may begin with a Level 2 gym qualification and then become a personal trainer, adding specialist qualifications as they progress. Each route needs a different qualification, and each leads to different types of employment.
MeritB.M3

Analyse recruitment processes, making links to their effectiveness in chosen career pathways.

The job description for a sports development officer asks for experience of community projects and a relevant qualification. A strong application shows evidence of project work, linked to the community outcomes the role needs. The interview then tests the same skills through a scenario question. This process is effective because it checks both the qualifications and the practical skills the role requires, which matters for a job that depends on working with communities.
DistinctionC.D3

Evaluate how career planning strategies can support long-term progression.

A career plan that sets short-term goals, such as completing a coaching award within six months, gives a clear first step. Medium-term goals, such as gaining experience with a team, build the evidence employers look for. Long-term goals, such as leading a development programme, give direction. The strategy works best when reviewed regularly, because roles and qualifications change. Its main limitation is that a plan can become rigid, so it should be adjusted when new opportunities arise.
7

Teaching and assessment tips

Tips
  • Use job descriptions and person specifications as the basis for the skills audit. Every point in the audit should map to something in the specification.
  • For safeguarding and health and safety, name the principle and the professional responsibility that goes with it, such as reporting a concern.
  • For the career plan, make each goal specific and dated. "Get a coaching qualification by March" is far stronger than "improve my coaching".
  • Use real pathways from the country where the student is studying, and say where the examples come from.
Common mistakes
  • Listing careers without explaining the qualifications or progression routes they need.
  • Writing a skills audit that does not link to the role's person specification.
  • Setting goals that are general or impossible to measure, so progress cannot be checked.

Assessment approach

This unit is assessed through a Pearson Set Assignment Brief (PSAB). Centres must use the PSAB specified for the relevant assessment series without alteration or contextualisation. Two PSABs are available for each series, with an advised period of 20 hours.

Full information on assessment, resubmissions and retakes is in the BTEC Teacher Hub.

Key terms

Person specificationThe qualities, skills and experience an employer requires from a candidate.
Continuing Professional Development (CPD)Ongoing learning that keeps a professional up to date in their role.
SafeguardingProtecting children and vulnerable adults from harm, abuse, neglect or exploitation.
Duty of careThe legal and professional obligation to take reasonable steps to keep others safe.
Zero-hours contractAn employment contract in which the employer does not guarantee a set number of hours.

Reliability

Whether a test gives consistent, repeatable results — one of the two essential qualities of any worthwhile fitness test.

📖What it means

Reliability is the extent to which a test produces consistent, repeatable results. A reliable test, if repeated under the same conditions, will give the same or very similar results each time.

Reliability depends on standardising the test conditions — the same equipment, procedure, instructions, tester and environment — so that any change in the result reflects a real change in the performer, not a change in how the test was run.

It is distinct from validity (whether a test measures what it claims to). A test can be reliable but not valid, and a good fitness test must be both — consistent and measuring the right thing. Reliability is essential for meaningfully monitoring progress over time.

🏅Sporting examples

Standardised conditions

To keep a bleep test reliable, a coach uses the same audio track, surface, instructions and procedure every time, so changes in score reflect real fitness changes.

Monitoring

A reliable test lets a coach compare an athlete's results across a season with confidence that differences are genuine.

Reliable but not valid

A test could give very consistent results (reliable) yet still not measure the intended component of fitness (not valid).

✍️Using the term in context

Define reliability and distinguish it from validity:

"Reliability is the extent to which a test gives consistent, repeatable results under standardised conditions; it is distinct from validity, and a good fitness test must be both reliable and valid."

Stress that standardising conditions is what makes a test reliable.

A.1.1 Theme A · Communication SL & HL

Inter-system Communication

How the nervous and endocrine systems send messages around the body, and how they work together to prepare you for, and control you during, exercise.

1Overview 2Presentation 3Deep Dive 4Exam Tips 5Video 6Practice Questions
1

Overview

Your body has two communication systems. The nervous system sends fast electrical signals along nerves. The endocrine system releases hormones into the blood, which act more slowly but last longer. In sport, the two systems work together: the nervous system gives instant control, while hormones keep the body's response going for as long as the exercise lasts.

By the end of this topic you should be able to

  • Outline the organisation of the nervous system.
  • Describe how a nerve impulse travels and crosses to a muscle.
  • Outline how the endocrine system communicates using hormones.
  • Compare nervous and endocrine communication.
  • Explain how both systems work together before and during exercise.

Key Terms

NeuronA nerve cell that carries electrical impulses.
Action potentialThe electrical impulse that travels along a neuron.
NeurotransmitterA chemical released at a synapse to pass a signal on.
Neuromuscular junctionThe synapse between a motor neuron and a muscle fibre.
HormoneA chemical messenger released by a gland into the blood.
Target cellA cell with receptors for a specific hormone.

Deep Dive

How the nervous system is organised

The central nervous system (CNS) is the brain and spinal cord. It processes information and makes decisions. The peripheral nervous system (PNS) is every nerve outside the CNS. It carries information in from the senses (sensory neurons) and out to muscles and glands (motor neurons).

The motor side splits into the somatic system, which controls voluntary skeletal muscle, and the autonomic system, which controls involuntary actions like heart rate. The autonomic system has two branches. The sympathetic branch prepares the body for action, and the parasympathetic branch calms it back down.

How the nervous system is organised. The sympathetic branch prepares the body for exercise; the parasympathetic branch returns it to rest.

How a nerve impulse reaches a muscle

A motor neuron carries an action potential, a wave of electrical charge, from the spinal cord along its axon. A fatty myelin sheath insulates the axon. The impulse jumps between the small gaps in the sheath, called the nodes of Ranvier, so it travels much faster than it would along an uninsulated axon.

When the impulse reaches the end of the neuron, it can't jump the tiny gap to the muscle. Instead, the neuron releases the neurotransmitter acetylcholine (ACh). ACh crosses the gap at the neuromuscular junction, binds to receptors on the muscle fibre, and triggers a new action potential in the muscle that causes it to contract. The enzyme acetylcholinesterase then breaks ACh down, so the muscle fibre can relax until the next impulse arrives.

A: a motor neuron carrying an impulse to a muscle fibre. B: how acetylcholine carries the signal across the neuromuscular junction.

How the endocrine system communicates

Endocrine glands release hormones straight into the blood. The blood carries them everywhere, but they only affect target cells that have the right receptors. Key glands in sport include:

  • Adrenal medulla: adrenaline and noradrenaline raise heart rate and release glucose for energy.
  • Pancreas: insulin lowers blood glucose, and glucagon raises it.
  • Adrenal cortex: cortisol helps the body use fat and protein for fuel during long exercise.
  • Pituitary gland: growth hormone supports tissue growth and repair.

Nervous vs endocrine communication

Nervous systemEndocrine system
SignalElectrical impulses plus neurotransmittersHormones (chemicals)
RouteAlong nervesIn the bloodstream
SpeedVery fast (milliseconds)Slower (seconds to minutes)
DurationShort-livedLonger-lasting
TargetSpecific muscle or glandAny cell with the right receptor

Working together: a 400 m race

Before the start: anticipation activates the sympathetic nervous system, so heart rate rises before you move (the anticipatory rise). Sympathetic nerves also stimulate the adrenal medulla to release adrenaline.

During the race: sympathetic nerves raise heart rate and the force of each beat instantly. Adrenaline reinforces this and keeps it going, and it also helps release glucose from glycogen stores to fuel the working muscles.

After the race: the parasympathetic nervous system takes over and slows heart rate back towards resting levels, while hormone levels gradually fall.

Exam Tips

🎯
Name the specific hormone and gland"Adrenaline from the adrenal medulla" scores. "A hormone is released" doesn't.
⚖️
Make comparisons point by pointFor "compare" or "distinguish" questions, pair each point with its opposite: "Nervous signals are fast, whereas hormonal signals are slower."
🏃
Always add a sporting example"Explain" questions reward application. Link your answer to a real event such as a sprint start or a penalty kick.
📖
Read the command term"Outline" needs brief points. "Explain" needs a reason or mechanism for each point. Check the IB command terms.
⚠️
Common mistakeDon't say the impulse "jumps to the muscle". It's carried across the neuromuscular junction by acetylcholine, a chemical.

Practice Questions

1Outline two differences between nervous and endocrine communication.2 marksAnswer
Model answer — any two of
  • Nervous signals travel along nerves, whereas hormones travel in the blood.
  • Nervous communication is faster than endocrine communication.
  • Nervous effects are short-lived, whereas hormonal effects last longer.
  • Nervous signals reach a specific target, whereas hormones can affect any cell with the right receptor.
2Distinguish between the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems.2 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • The sympathetic system prepares the body for action, for example by increasing heart rate.
  • The parasympathetic system returns the body to rest, for example by decreasing heart rate.
3Describe the role of acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • The action potential reaches the end of the motor neuron and acetylcholine is released.
  • Acetylcholine crosses the gap and binds to receptors on the muscle fibre.
  • This triggers an action potential in the muscle fibre, causing it to contract.
4Outline how an action potential travels along a myelinated motor neuron.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • The impulse is a wave of electrical charge (depolarisation) along the axon.
  • The myelin sheath insulates the axon.
  • The impulse jumps between gaps in the myelin sheath, so it travels faster.
5Explain how the nervous and endocrine systems work together to increase heart rate before and during a 400 m race.4 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Before the race, anticipation activates the sympathetic nervous system, raising heart rate (the anticipatory rise).
  • Sympathetic nerves stimulate the adrenal medulla to release adrenaline.
  • During the race, sympathetic nerves rapidly increase heart rate and the force of contraction.
  • Adrenaline in the blood reinforces and sustains the raised heart rate for the rest of the race.

VO₂ Max & Aerobic Capacity

The single most cited number in endurance sport — the maximum rate at which your body can take in, transport and use oxygen. Here's what it really measures, and what it doesn't.

Key Terms

VO₂ MaxThe maximum volume of oxygen a person can take in, transport and use per minute during maximal exercise.
Aerobic CapacityThe ability to take in, transport and use oxygen to sustain prolonged exercise.
Relative VO₂ MaxVO₂ max expressed per kilogram of body mass (ml/kg/min), allowing fair comparison.
Direct MeasurementMeasuring VO₂ max in a lab using a gas analyser during a maximal test.
Indirect EstimationEstimating VO₂ max from a field test such as the Multistage Fitness (Bleep) Test or Cooper Test.

What VO₂ Max Measures

VO₂ max is the maximum rate at which the body can take in, transport and use oxygen. It is the standard measure of aerobic capacity and reflects the combined effectiveness of the respiratory, cardiovascular and muscular systems. It is usually expressed relative to body mass (ml/kg/min) so athletes of different sizes can be compared fairly.

Factors Affecting VO₂ Max

  • Training — endurance training raises VO₂ max through adaptations such as increased stroke volume, capillary and mitochondrial density
  • Genetics — a large part of VO₂ max is genetically determined and sets an upper ceiling
  • Age — VO₂ max tends to decline with age
  • Sex — average values differ, partly due to differences in haemoglobin and body composition
  • Body composition — a higher proportion of lean mass is associated with a higher relative VO₂ max

Measuring VO₂ Max

VO₂ max can be measured directly in a laboratory using a gas analyser during a maximal test — the most accurate method, but requiring equipment and maximal effort. It can also be estimated indirectly from field tests such as the Multistage Fitness (Bleep) Test or the Cooper 12-minute run, which are cheap and practical but less precise.

Exam tip: A complete evaluation names a specific test and comments on its validity/practicality — e.g. direct lab testing is accurate but requires maximal effort and equipment, whereas the Bleep Test is practical but only an estimate.

Uses and Limitations

VO₂ max is one of the best single predictors of endurance potential, but it has limits: it reflects a ceiling rather than the intensity actually sustainable, and it is strongly genetically capped. This is why the lactate inflection point is often a better predictor of actual endurance performance.

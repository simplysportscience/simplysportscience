C.3.3 Theme C · Motivation SL & HL

Motivational Climate

How the psychological environment created by the coach shapes whether athletes focus on mastery and improvement or on comparison and winning.

1Overview 2Presentation 3Deep Dive 4Exam Tips 5Video 6Practice Questions
1

Overview

A motivational climate is the psychological environment that a coach (and the wider team) creates through session design, instructions, feedback, rewards and the way mistakes are handled. Climates are commonly described as mastery (task-involving) or ego (performance-involving). A mastery climate asks "How can we improve?" and values effort, learning and cooperation. An ego climate asks "Who is the best?" and values winning and outperforming others. The climate influences the goal orientation of each athlete, and through it their effort, teamwork, anxiety and enjoyment. The TARGET framework gives coaches six levers to build a mastery climate.

By the end of this topic you should be able to

  • Define motivational climate as the psychological environment created by the coach.
  • Compare mastery and ego climates using effort, improvement, winning and comparison.
  • Explain why mastery climates support enjoyment, teamwork and long-term participation.
  • Explain why ego climates are linked to anxiety and dropout.
  • Apply the TARGET framework to show how a coach can foster a mastery climate.

Key Terms

Motivational climateThe psychological environment created by the coach through session design, instructions and feedback.
Mastery (task-involving) climateAn environment that emphasises personal or team development, effort and improvement.
Ego (performance-involving) climateAn environment that emphasises winning and outperforming others.
Goal orientationThe way an individual defines success, as task (self-referenced) or ego (comparison with others).
TARGETA framework of six levers (Task, Authority, Recognition, Grouping, Evaluation, Time) that shape the climate.
RecognitionThe way praise and rewards are given, to individuals or to the team.

Deep Dive

What is a motivational climate?

A motivational climate is not a personality trait. It is the environment that the coach creates, and it shapes how athletes judge success and failure. A coach creates it through what they choose to measure, how they give feedback, who receives praise, how mistakes are treated, and how decisions are made. Because athletes respond to the climate, the coach's choices affect the whole group, not only one athlete.

Mastery and ego climates compared

In a mastery climate, success is defined by personal progress, skill development, effort and learning. Mistakes are seen as information for improvement, and recognition goes to effort and cooperation. In an ego climate, success is defined by outperforming others, mistakes are a reason for blame or embarrassment, and recognition goes to the top performer. The table below sets out these differences.

Mastery (task) and ego (performance) climates compared across several aspects. Mastery climates are linked to enjoyment, persistence and teamwork; ego climates are linked to anxiety and dropout risk.

Mastery climates are linked to enjoyment, cooperation, sustained effort and better long-term development. Ego climates can produce short-term results for some athletes, but they are more likely to be linked to anxiety, fear of making mistakes, rivalry within the team and dropping out, especially for athletes who do not feel they are succeeding.

A useful exam point is that a climate can be strong even when it is not stated. A coach who only rewards the top scorer, publicly compares players and puts winning above learning creates an ego climate, even if they never use the word "winning" in their instructions.

The TARGET framework

TARGET sets out six areas in which coaches can structure instruction and practice to build a mastery climate: Task (the design and challenge of activities), Authority (how much athletes share in decisions), Recognition (how praise and rewards are given), Grouping (how athletes are organised), Evaluation (how progress and mistakes are judged) and Time (how much time is given to learn and how the pace is set).

The TARGET framework, with a mastery approach and an ego approach for each of the six elements. A mastery climate is built by applying the TARGET elements consistently.

How the climate shapes behaviour and outcomes

The climate influences the goal orientation that athletes adopt, and this shapes their behaviour. In a mastery climate, athletes tend to put in effort, cooperate, and keep trying after mistakes. In an ego climate, athletes may compare themselves with others, fear failure, and avoid risks that could help them learn. Over time these behaviours produce different outcomes: enjoyment, teamwork and long-term participation in the mastery pathway, and anxiety, reduced enjoyment and dropout risk in the ego pathway.

The flow from motivational climate, to athlete goal orientation, to behaviour and outcomes. Individual differences also affect how an athlete responds to the same climate.

Applying the idea in team sport

In team sports, a mastery climate can improve teamwork, because athletes are encouraged to cooperate and to value the team's development rather than only their own statistics. A coach who asks players to help each other learn a tactic, and recognises the support given to teammates, is building a mastery climate. Ego-focused selection and public ranking can damage team cohesion, because athletes may focus on their own position rather than the group.

The climate can also be shaped by parents, peers and the wider environment, but the coach has the most direct influence through the choices made in each session.

Exam Tips

🎯
Define the climate as the coach's environmentUse the phrase "psychological environment created by the coach through session design, instructions and feedback". Check the IB command terms.
⚖️
Compare with the same terms each timeUse effort, improvement, winning and comparison for both climates, so that your comparison is clear and balanced.
🧩
Apply TARGET by nameFor questions on how a coach can create a mastery climate, name the TARGET element (for example Recognition) and give a specific coaching action for it.
🔗
Link the climate to outcomesExplain the chain: climate → goal orientation → behaviour → outcomes, such as enjoyment, teamwork, anxiety or dropout.
⚠️
Common mistakeDon't say that an ego climate always produces poor results. It can work for some athletes in the short term, but it is linked to anxiety and dropout. Also, don't confuse the climate with the individual athlete's goal orientation: they are linked, but they are different.

Practice Questions

Write your answer first, then open the model answer to check it.

1Define motivational climate.1 markAnswer
Model answer
  • The psychological environment created by the coach through session design, instructions and feedback.
2State two coaching behaviours that foster a mastery climate.2 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Praising effort, improvement and learning rather than only the final result.
  • Treating mistakes as information for improvement, and giving athletes a role in decisions. (Any two, for example from TARGET.)
3Compare a mastery climate with an ego climate in terms of how success is defined and how mistakes are treated.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • In a mastery climate, success is defined by personal progress, effort and learning; in an ego climate it is defined by outperforming others or winning.
  • In a mastery climate mistakes are information for improvement; in an ego climate mistakes are a reason for blame or embarrassment.
  • The mastery climate therefore supports persistence and learning, while the ego climate can increase fear of failure.
4Explain how a coach could use the TARGET framework to make a basketball training session more mastery-focused.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Task: set varied, challenging drills with clear skill goals, rather than one drill judged only on the result.
  • Recognition: praise effort and improvement for all players, not only the top scorer.
  • Evaluation: measure each athlete against their own previous performance, and treat errors as learning opportunities.
5Discuss how a motivational climate can affect the enjoyment, teamwork and long-term participation of athletes.4 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • A mastery climate encourages effort, cooperation and learning, which supports enjoyment and teamwork.
  • Athletes in a mastery climate are more likely to persist after setbacks, which supports long-term participation.
  • An ego climate can increase rivalry and fear of failure, which may reduce enjoyment and lead to anxiety or dropout, especially for athletes who feel they are not succeeding.
  • The climate is shaped by the coach, so coaches are responsible for choosing approaches that support all athletes, not only the best performers.

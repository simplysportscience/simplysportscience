Biomechanics

The physics behind every throw, spin, jump and tackle — matched point-for-point to the real 8386 syllabus.

Key Terms

Linear MotionMovement of a body in a straight or curved line, all parts moving the same distance, direction and time.
Angular MotionMovement of a body in a circular path about an axis of rotation.
Moment of InertiaA body's resistance to a change in rotational motion; depends on mass and its distribution from the axis.
Parabolic FlightA symmetrical flight path, e.g. a shot put, where air resistance has minimal effect.
Centre of MassThe point at which a body's mass is evenly balanced in all directions.

2.1 Linear Motion

Linear motion is movement of a body in a straight or curved line, where all parts move the same distance, in the same direction, over the same time — created by an external force passing through the centre of mass.

Key quantities: distance and speed are scalar (magnitude only); displacement, velocity, acceleration and momentum are vector (magnitude and direction).

Exam tip: You must be able to sketch and interpret graphs of distance, displacement, speed, velocity and time — this is explicitly examinable, not just definitions.

2.2 Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's Three Laws of Motion

Force, mass, weight, inertia and acceleration all interact to create and affect linear motion. Forces acting during physical activity include gravitational force/weight, air resistance, friction, reaction, and action/muscular force — which can be balanced or unbalanced.

  • First law (inertia) — a body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by an external force.
  • Second law (acceleration) — acceleration is proportional to the force applied and inversely proportional to mass.
  • Third law (reaction) — for every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force.

2.3 Angular Motion

Angular motion is movement of a body in a circular path about an axis of rotation, created by a force passing outside the centre of mass. Key quantities are angular momentum, moment of inertia and angular velocity, linked by the equation: angular momentum = moment of inertia × angular velocity.

Moment of inertia depends on the mass of the body and the distribution of that mass from the axis of rotation. The principle of conservation of angular momentum explains why a diver or gymnast spins faster when tucking (reducing moment of inertia) and slower when extending.

Exam tip: You must be able to state and apply the actual equation linking angular momentum, moment of inertia and angular velocity — not just describe the relationship in words.

2.4 Parabolic and Non-Parabolic Flight

A projectile's flight has horizontal and vertical vector components. A parabolic flight path (e.g. a shot put) is symmetrical, since air resistance has minimal effect on a dense object; a non-parabolic path (e.g. a badminton shuttlecock) is asymmetrical, heavily affected by air resistance.

Horizontal displacement is affected by three factors: height of release, speed of release, and angle of release.

2.5 Properties of Bodies and Objects

Centre of mass is the point at which a body's mass is evenly balanced in all directions, and its position can shift depending on body position (e.g. a Fosbury flop). Stability is affected by:

  • Area of the base of support
  • Height of the centre of mass
  • Position of the centre of mass above the base of support (the line of gravity relative to the base)
  • Mass of the body

Exam tip: "Explain the factors affecting stability" answers should reference all four factors together with a specific sporting example (e.g. a rugby scrum, a gymnast's handstand) — partial answers naming only one or two factors lose marks.

Practice Check
1. Explain Newton's first law of motion using a sporting example. +
Model answer points:
  • A body remains at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.
  • Example: a stationary shot put remains at rest until the thrower applies a force to it.
2. Explain the principle of conservation of angular momentum using a practical example. +
Model answer points:
  • Angular momentum (moment of inertia × angular velocity) remains constant once a performer is airborne, with no external torque acting.
  • A diver tucking reduces moment of inertia, so angular velocity increases to conserve angular momentum, increasing spin rate.
  • Extending the body before entry increases moment of inertia, slowing rotation.
3. Explain the factors that affect the horizontal displacement of a thrown object. +
Model answer points:
  • Height of release — a greater release height increases horizontal distance, all else equal.
  • Speed of release — greater speed increases horizontal distance.
  • Angle of release — affects the balance between horizontal and vertical distance travelled.
4. Explain the factors affecting the stability of a rugby player in a scrum. +
Model answer points:
  • A larger base of support (feet positioned wide) increases stability.
  • A lower centre of mass increases stability.
  • Keeping the centre of mass positioned directly above the base of support (line of gravity within the base) increases stability.
  • Greater body mass increases stability, requiring more force to be displaced.

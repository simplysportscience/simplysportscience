Newton's Laws of Motion

Three laws written in the 1600s explain every start, collision and jump in sport. Master them and you can analyse almost any movement.

Key Terms

InertiaThe resistance of a body to a change in its state of motion; proportional to its mass.
ForceA push or pull that can change a body’s state of motion; measured in newtons (N).
MomentumThe product of a body’s mass and velocity (p = m × v).
AccelerationThe rate of change of velocity; proportional to the net force applied (a = F/m).
Ground Reaction ForceThe equal and opposite force the ground exerts on a body pushing against it.

Newton's First Law — Inertia

A body will remain at rest, or continue moving at constant velocity, unless acted upon by an external force. A body's inertia — its reluctance to change motion — depends on its mass. A stationary ball stays still until kicked; a moving bobsleigh keeps sliding until friction and air resistance slow it.

Newton's Second Law — Acceleration

The acceleration of a body is proportional to the force applied and inversely proportional to its mass (F = m × a). A larger force produces greater acceleration; a heavier object accelerates less for the same force. This law explains why a sprinter drives hard against the blocks to accelerate, and why force applied through the centre of mass determines the change in momentum.

Exam tip: For "explain" questions, quote F = ma and apply it to the specific example — e.g. "a greater force from the leg muscles produces a greater acceleration of the sprinter's body mass."

Newton's Third Law — Action-Reaction

For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. When an athlete pushes down and back into the ground, the ground pushes up and forward on them with equal force — the ground reaction force — propelling them forward. This law underpins running, jumping and swimming.

Applying the Laws Together

LawStatementSporting example
First (inertia)Stays at rest / constant velocity unless a force actsA hockey ball stays still until struck
Second (acceleration)F = m × aA greater force accelerates a sprinter faster off the blocks
Third (reaction)Equal and opposite reactionA sprinter drives into the blocks; the blocks drive them forward

Practice Check
1. State Newton's first law of motion and give a sporting example. +
Model answer points:
  • A body remains at rest or moving at constant velocity unless acted upon by an external force.
  • Example: a stationary football remains still until a force (a kick) is applied.
2. Using Newton's second law, explain how a sprinter accelerates out of the blocks. +
Model answer points:
  • Newton's second law states acceleration is proportional to force and inversely proportional to mass (F = ma).
  • The sprinter applies a large force against the blocks.
  • For their given body mass, this produces a large forward acceleration.
3. Explain how Newton's third law applies to a runner pushing off the ground. +
Model answer points:
  • Newton's third law states every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  • The runner pushes down and back into the ground (action).
  • The ground pushes up and forward on the runner with equal force (ground reaction force), propelling them forward.
4. Define inertia and explain how it relates to mass. +
Model answer points:
  • Inertia is a body's resistance to a change in its state of motion.
  • It is proportional to mass — a more massive body has greater inertia and is harder to start, stop or change direction.

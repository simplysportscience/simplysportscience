Guidance

The methods a coach uses to help a learner acquire a skill — visual, verbal, manual and mechanical, each with its own place.

📖What it means

Guidance refers to the methods a coach uses to help a performer learn or improve a skill. There are four main types, and the best choice depends on the learner and the skill.

Visual guidance (demonstrations, videos, diagrams) shows what the skill should look like. Verbal guidance (instructions, feedback) explains and directs. Manual guidance physically moves the performer through the movement. Mechanical guidance uses equipment (such as a harness or float) to support the action.

The type of guidance should match the stage of learning: beginners benefit greatly from visual and, at times, manual or mechanical guidance, while more advanced performers rely more on precise verbal guidance and their own feedback. Over-reliance on manual or mechanical guidance can hinder independent performance.

🏅Sporting examples

Visual

A coach demonstrates a tennis serve so a beginner can form a clear mental picture of the correct action.

Manual/mechanical

A gymnastics coach physically supports a learner through a somersault (manual), or uses a harness (mechanical), to build confidence and safety.

Verbal

An experienced athlete receives precise verbal guidance to fine-tune a specific aspect of their technique.

✍️Using the term in context

Name the four types and match them to the learner:

"The four types of guidance are visual, verbal, manual and mechanical; visual and manual guidance suit beginners forming a mental picture, while advanced performers rely more on precise verbal guidance, and over-reliance on manual guidance can hinder independence."

Justify a chosen type by the stage of learning and the skill.

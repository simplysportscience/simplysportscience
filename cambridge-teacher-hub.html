AS Level Sport & Physical Education · 8386

Teacher Hub

How the qualification is structured, assessed and graded, and how to plan for it.

1

Qualification overview

Cambridge International AS Level Sport & Physical Education (8386) is an AS Level qualification. It is designed for about 180 guided learning hours. Students are eligible for grades a to e, and performance below grade e is reported as ungraded.

This hub is based on the 2024 to 2026 syllabus. Cambridge has published the 2027 to 2029 syllabus, which keeps the same structure and assessment. Check the latest syllabus before you plan, and check the samples database for submission details in each series.

3

Coursework

Candidates choose two physical activities from the syllabus list, and each is marked out of 35. Performance is assessed in competitive conditioned practices and in formal competitive games, competitions or situations. Each activity is assessed against four strands, using the generic criteria and the guidance for that activity.

Log of competitive participation

A log is required for every candidate. It shows the level and frequency of competition in each of the two activities. Training sessions are not included.

Filming and evidence

  • The moderator must be able to identify the candidate.
  • Evidence may be edited to focus on the candidate, but must not be edited to make the candidate look more consistent or able than they are.
  • Speed settings of filmed evidence must not be adjusted.

Moderation and submission

Centres mark internally. If more than one teacher marks, internal moderation makes sure marking is consistent. Cambridge then moderates a sample externally. Follow the instructions for Coursework 8386/02 on the samples database for each series.

UngradedPerformance did not reach grade e. Reported on the statement of results, not the certificate.
  • Candidates can retake the AS Level as many times as they want.
  • Marks from Component 2 can be carried forward to future series, subject to the rules in the syllabus.
  • Grade descriptions show the standard typically achieved at each grade. A weakness in one area can be balanced by strength in another.
5

Planning and teaching

The subject content is organised into three themes and 12 topics. Use the topic pages to plan your sequence, and check each learning outcome for the depth it requires.

  • Command words: the syllabus defines 20 command words. Teach these early, since they determine how students write their answers.
  • Mathematical requirements: the syllabus lists the maths students need, including units, graphs and basic statistics. Build these into Component 1 practice.
  • Practical teaching: plan competitive conditioned practices and formal games for both activities, and start the log from the first competition.
  • Past papers and specimen material: use Cambridge's specimen papers, mark schemes and past papers from cambridgeinternational.org. Use examiner reports to check the standard expected.
  • Generative AI: follow Cambridge's policy for coursework, and make sure students understand what counts as their own work.

The syllabus notes that availability of this course varies by centre location. Check the availability section of the syllabus before you enter students.

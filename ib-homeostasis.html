Homeostasis

Exercise is a constant threat to the body's internal balance. Homeostasis is how the body fights back — sensing change and correcting it, moment by moment.

Key Terms

HomeostasisThe maintenance of a constant internal environment despite changes in external or internal conditions.
Negative FeedbackA control mechanism that detects a change and triggers a response to reverse it back toward the norm.
ThermoregulationThe maintenance of core body temperature within a narrow, safe range.
Set PointThe optimal value of a variable that the body works to maintain.
EffectorA muscle or gland that carries out the response to correct a change.

What Is Homeostasis?

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a stable internal environment despite changing conditions. Variables such as core temperature, blood glucose, pH and hydration are all kept close to an optimal set point. Exercise disturbs many of these, so homeostatic control is constantly at work.

Negative Feedback

Most homeostatic control uses negative feedback. A receptor detects a change from the set point; a control centre processes the information; and an effector produces a response that reverses the change, returning the variable toward the set point. The response then switches off as normal conditions are restored.

Thermoregulation

Exercise generates large amounts of heat. To prevent core temperature rising dangerously, the body increases heat loss through vasodilation of skin blood vessels (radiation and convection) and sweating (evaporation). In cold conditions, vasoconstriction and shivering conserve and generate heat.

Exam tip: A strong answer names the specific effectors — sweat glands and skin arterioles — rather than just saying "the body cools down."

Blood Glucose Regulation

Blood glucose is maintained by the antagonistic hormones insulin (lowers glucose) and glucagon (raises glucose), secreted by the pancreas. During prolonged exercise, glucagon promotes the breakdown of liver glycogen to maintain glucose supply to the muscles — a clear example of negative feedback.

Practice Check
1. Define homeostasis. +
Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment despite changes in external or internal conditions.
2. Describe the stages of a negative feedback loop. +
Model answer points:
  • A receptor detects a change away from the set point.
  • A control centre processes the information.
  • An effector produces a response that reverses the change, returning the variable toward the set point.
3. Explain how the body maintains core temperature during intense exercise. +
Model answer points:
  • Exercise generates heat, raising core temperature above the set point.
  • Skin blood vessels vasodilate, increasing heat loss by radiation and convection.
  • Sweat glands secrete sweat, which cools the body through evaporation.
4. Explain why negative feedback is described as ‘self-limiting’. +
Model answer points:
  • The response acts to reverse the original change.
  • As the variable returns toward the set point, the stimulus is removed.
  • This switches off the response, preventing overcorrection.

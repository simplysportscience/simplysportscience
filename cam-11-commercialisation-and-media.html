Commercialisation and the Media

How growth in media interest, sponsorship and professionalism have changed sport, and who benefits and who loses.

1Overview 2Presentation 3Deep Dive 4Exam Tips 5Video 6Practice Questions
1

Overview

Commercialisation is the process by which sport has become a business, driven by media coverage, advertising and sponsorship. This topic asks you to explain the factors that led to commercialisation, describe the golden triangle that links sponsors, the media and sport, and evaluate commercialisation for each group affected.

Specification content covered in this topic

  • Factors leading to commercialisation
  • The golden triangle
  • Evaluating commercialisation

Key Terms

CommercialisationThe process by which sport is organised and funded for commercial profit.
SponsorshipFinancial or other support from a business, in return for publicity and association with the sport or event.
Golden triangleThe relationship between sponsors or business, the media and sport, each of which depends on the others.
Primary spectatorA person who watches the event in person.
Secondary spectatorA remote audience who watches through the media.

Deep Dive

Factors leading to commercialisation

Sport has been commercialised because of growth in media interest, which has increased the value of broadcasting rights; growth in public interest and spectatorism, which increases the audience a sponsor can reach; professionalism, which has made performers paid employees; advertising, which pays for coverage and events; and sponsorship, which gives businesses access to the audience in return for funding.

Explain each factor and link it to the effect it has on sport, rather than listing them.

The golden triangle

The golden triangle describes the relationship between sponsorship and business, the media and sport. Sport provides the events and the audience; the media broadcast them and deliver the audience to sponsors; sponsors fund the sport and the media coverage. Each part depends on the others, and each can influence the others, for example by changing the timing of events to suit broadcasters.

Evaluating commercialisation

Commercialisation affects different groups in different ways, and your evaluation should consider each:

  • The performer or team: higher pay and profile, but more pressure, less control over schedules and greater exposure to criticism.
  • The sponsor or business: wider exposure and brand value, but cost and the risk of association with negative events.
  • The event or sport: funding, growth and professional standards, but changes to rules, timing and the character of the sport to suit broadcasters.
  • Primary spectators (at the event): better facilities and entertainment, but higher ticket prices and a less intimate atmosphere.
  • Secondary spectators (remote audience): access to events they could not otherwise see, but a viewing experience shaped by commentary and advertising.

Reach a judgement that considers which group gains most, and which bears the greatest cost.

Practice Questions

Write your answer first, then open the model answer to check it.

1Explain how sponsorship has contributed to the commercialisation of sport.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Sponsors provide money for events and performers, in return for publicity.
  • This allows sport to become more professional and better funded.
  • Sport in turn gives sponsors an audience and an association with success.
2Explain what is meant by the golden triangle.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • The golden triangle is the relationship between sponsors or business, the media and sport.
  • Each depends on the others: sport provides the events, the media broadcast them, and sponsors fund both.
  • Each can influence the others, for example by changing event timing.
3Evaluate the effect of commercialisation on primary spectators.4 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Benefit: better facilities, entertainment and services at the event.
  • Limitation: higher ticket prices can exclude some people.
  • Limitation: scheduling for broadcasters can change the event and the atmosphere.
  • Judgement: the benefits depend on whether the price and experience are accessible to the wider audience.

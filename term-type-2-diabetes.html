Type 2 Diabetes

Impaired blood glucose control — a condition strongly linked to inactivity that physical activity can help prevent and manage.

📖What it means

Type 2 diabetes is a metabolic condition in which the body cannot regulate blood glucose effectively, either because cells become resistant to insulin or because insufficient insulin is produced. Blood glucose remains elevated.

It is strongly associated with physical inactivity, obesity and poor diet, making it largely preventable and manageable through lifestyle change. Unlike type 1, it is not an autoimmune condition and is typically adult-onset.

Regular physical activity is one of the most effective strategies for both preventing and managing type 2 diabetes — it improves insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake in the muscles, helping control blood glucose. This is a key health benefit of active lifestyles.

🏅Sporting examples

Lifestyle link

Type 2 diabetes is strongly associated with inactivity, obesity and poor diet — all modifiable risk factors.

Exercise benefit

Regular aerobic exercise improves insulin sensitivity, helping manage blood glucose in type 2 diabetes.

Prevention

Meeting physical activity guidelines significantly reduces the risk of developing type 2 diabetes.

✍️Using the term in context

Define type 2 diabetes and the role of exercise:

"Type 2 diabetes is impaired blood glucose control due to insulin resistance, strongly linked to inactivity and obesity; regular physical activity improves insulin sensitivity and glucose control, making it a key prevention and management strategy."

Note it is largely preventable through lifestyle.

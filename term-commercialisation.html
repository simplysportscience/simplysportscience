Commercialisation

How modern sport became big business — the growing influence of money, media and marketing, with both benefits and drawbacks.

📖What it means

Commercialisation is the process by which sport has increasingly become driven by profit and business interests, through sponsorship, broadcasting rights, advertising and merchandising. It links sport tightly with the media and commerce — often called the 'golden triangle' of sport, sponsorship and media.

It brings clear benefits: greater funding for athletes and clubs, improved facilities, wider media coverage, and increased global exposure that can grow participation and revenue.

It also brings drawbacks: sport may be shaped to suit sponsors and broadcasters rather than participants — rule changes, altered scheduling, over-commercialisation, and pressure that can encourage deviance such as doping to meet financial expectations.

🏅Sporting examples

Broadcasting

Television deals have reshaped scheduling and even rules in many sports to make them more marketable and viewer-friendly.

Sponsorship

Elite athletes and teams rely on sponsorship income, which funds their sport but can also create pressure and conflicts of interest.

Balanced view

Commercialisation has funded better facilities and wider coverage, but critics argue it can prioritise profit over the interests of grassroots participants.

✍️Using the term in context

Give a balanced account with benefits and drawbacks:

"Commercialisation links sport with media and business through sponsorship and broadcasting; it increases funding and exposure, but can shape sport to suit commercial interests and create pressure that may encourage deviance."

Reference the 'golden triangle' of sport, media and sponsorship where relevant.

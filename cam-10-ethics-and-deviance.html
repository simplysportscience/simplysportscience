Topic 10 Theme 3 · Sociocultural issues and technology AS Level 8386

Ethics and Deviance

Performance-enhancing drugs, violence and competition manipulation: their causes, consequences and the strategies used to reduce them.

1Overview 2Presentation 3Deep Dive 4Exam Tips 5Video 6Practice Questions
1

Overview

Ethics and deviance examine behaviour that breaks the rules or the spirit of sport. This topic covers three areas: the use of prohibited performance-enhancing drugs, violence by performers and spectators, and competition manipulation. For each, you need to explain the causes, the consequences for the individual, the sport and society, and the strategies used to reduce the problem, which you must evaluate.

Specification content covered in this topic

  • 10.1 Performance-enhancing drugs (PEDs)
  • 10.2 Violence
  • 10.3 Competition manipulation

Key Terms

Performance-enhancing drug (PED)A substance or method, prohibited by sporting bodies, used to improve performance.
ViolencePhysical or verbal aggression that causes harm, by performers or spectators.
Competition manipulationCheating by performers, coaches or officials that affects the outcome of a match or event.
Match-fixingAn arrangement to determine the result of a match in advance.
TankingDeliberately losing a match.

Deep Dive

10.1 Performance-enhancing drugs (PEDs)

Performers use prohibited PEDs for several reasons, which you should explain using the specific causes in the specification: pressure to win; to increase fitness such as strength or endurance; to reduce weight or body fat; to speed up recovery after training or injury; to increase the intensity of training; to reduce the effects of fatigue; to increase concentration; to participate despite injury or to mask pain; to hide the presence of other drugs; and because of a belief that others are using them.

The consequences are health risks and damage to the performer, the loss of fair competition and public trust in the sport, and the influence on young people who see role models using drugs.

Strategies to reduce use include testing, sanctions, education and anti-doping programmes. Evaluate each: testing is effective at detection but can be avoided by new drugs; education can change attitudes but takes time; sanctions deter but cannot remove the pressure to win.

10.2 Violence

Violence can be caused by performers (for example through frustration, pressure to win, or a belief that aggression is part of the game) and by spectators at the event (for example through rivalry, alcohol, or group identity). Explain the cause for each group.

The consequences are injury and legal action for the individual, damage to the reputation and finances of the sport, and public disorder and cost to society.

Strategies to prevent violence include sanctions for performers, education, stewarding and ticketing, segregation of rival supporters, and CCTV. Evaluate each one: stewarding and CCTV can deter, but they cannot change the attitudes that cause violence.

10.3 Competition manipulation

Competition manipulation is cheating by performers, coaches or officials that affects the outcome of a match or event, and removes the unpredictability that makes competition valuable. The forms are betting (wagers linked to the result), match-fixing (an arrangement to fix the result), insider information (using private knowledge to gain an advantage), and tanking (deliberately losing a match).

Strategies to reduce manipulation include monitoring of betting patterns, sanctions, education for performers and officials, and integrity units within sporting bodies. Evaluate each by considering how easily it can be avoided and how much it protects the unpredictability of the sport.

Exam Tips

🎯
Exam tipFor causes, name the specific reason from the list and explain how it leads to the behaviour. Do not list causes without explaining them.
🧠
Exam tipFor consequences, name the group affected (the individual, the sport or society) for each consequence.
📐
Exam tipFor evaluation of strategies, weigh what the strategy achieves against what it cannot change, and reach a judgement.
🔁
Exam tipUse the exact terms for forms of competition manipulation: match-fixing, tanking, insider information and betting.

Practice Questions

Write your answer first, then open the model answer to check it.

1Explain two reasons why a performer might use a prohibited performance-enhancing drug.4 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • To increase fitness, such as strength or endurance, to gain an advantage.
  • To speed up recovery after injury, so that the performer can return to competition sooner.
  • Pressure to win is a motivation, as the performer feels they must succeed.
  • Each reason is explained in terms of the advantage it gives the performer.
2Explain the consequences of the use of prohibited PEDs for society.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Young people may copy the behaviour of role models who use drugs, because they admire them.
  • Public trust in sport and its values is damaged.
  • Health services may face costs from the health problems caused by drug use.
3Explain how tanking affects the unpredictability of competition.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Tanking is deliberately losing a match, so the outcome is decided before the match is played.
  • This removes the unpredictability of the contest, which is one of the values that makes competition valuable.
  • Spectators and sponsors lose confidence in the result.
4Evaluate the use of sanctions as a strategy to prevent violence by spectators.4 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Sanctions such as bans and fines deter spectators who want to avoid the penalty.
  • They are effective where the spectator can be identified, through CCTV and ticketing.
  • However, sanctions only act after violence has occurred and do not change the attitudes that cause it.
  • Sanctions are therefore most effective when combined with education and stewarding.

🧠 Revision

How to Revise Sport Science

The methods that work best for exam courses are not the ones that feel most comfortable. Re-reading notes feels productive, but it is one of the weakest ways to remember. These are the habits that make a difference.

1. Test yourself, don't just re-read

Trying to recall information from memory is one of the most effective ways to make it stick. After each topic, close the page and write down what you remember, then check and fill the gaps. Use the Practice Questions at the end of each topic page for the same purpose: answer first, then open the model answer.

Try thisAfter reading a topic, write three questions you think an examiner could ask about it. Answer them without looking, then check.

2. Space it out

Revising a topic once, the night before, is much less effective than revisiting it several times over a longer period. Study a topic, leave it for a few days, then go back to it. Each return is harder than the last, and that difficulty is what makes the memory stronger.

  • Plan short sessions across the week rather than one long one before the exam.
  • Return to topics from earlier in the course, not just the ones you covered last.
  • Keep a simple list of topics with dates you last revised them.

3. Learn the command words

Examiners use command terms to tell you what kind of answer they want. Knowing exactly what "evaluate", "explain" or "outline" requires before you write saves time and marks. Each term is explained, with example questions and model answers, on the command terms guide.

4. Use real sporting examples

Examiners reward application, not just definitions. When you learn a concept, ask yourself where it appears in a sport you know. A definition of ground reaction force becomes much easier to remember when you can picture a sprinter pushing off the blocks. Writing one example for each key idea is one of the fastest ways to improve your answers.

5. Watch first, then read

Start with the video or presentation for the big picture, then move to the topic page for the detail. Seeing the whole topic first makes the detail easier to place.

6. Plan a revision session

A session with a clear plan works better than an open-ended one. A simple structure:

  1. Recall (5–10 minutes): write what you remember about the last topic you studied, without notes.
  2. Study (20–30 minutes): work through one topic or section, pausing to summarise each part in your own words.
  3. Practise (10–15 minutes): answer two or three practice questions, then check the model answers.
  4. Review (5 minutes): note what you found hardest, and plan when you will return to it.

7. Use the support pages

Two pages are worth returning to throughout the course:

  • IB Command Terms: what each term asks for, with worked examples.
  • Internal Assessment Support: stage-by-stage guidance for the scientific investigation, from research question to evaluation.

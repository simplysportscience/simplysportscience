The Cardiovascular System

How the heart, blood and vessels deliver oxygen to working muscles — and how the whole system ramps up the moment exercise begins.

Key Terms

Cardiac Output (Q)The volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute. Q = heart rate × stroke volume.
Stroke Volume (SV)The volume of blood ejected by the left ventricle per beat (~70 ml at rest).
Heart Rate (HR)The number of times the heart beats per minute.
Venous ReturnThe volume of blood returning to the heart via the veins.
Vascular ShuntThe redistribution of blood flow to working muscles during exercise via vasodilation and vasoconstriction.

Structure of the Heart

Heart Structure

The heart is a muscular double pump made of cardiac muscle (myocardium). The right side receives deoxygenated blood and pumps it to the lungs; the left side receives oxygenated blood and pumps it to the body. Because the left ventricle pumps against greater resistance, its wall is thicker.

Blood flows through four chambers — the right and left atria (receiving chambers) and the right and left ventricles (pumping chambers) — with valves ensuring one-way flow.

The Conduction System

Cardiac Conduction Pathway

The heart is myogenic — it generates its own electrical impulse. The sinoatrial node (SA node), the natural pacemaker, initiates each beat. The impulse spreads across the atria to the atrioventricular node (AV node), then down the bundle of His and through the Purkinje fibres, causing the ventricles to contract.

Cardiac Output, Stroke Volume and Heart Rate

Cardiac output is the product of heart rate and stroke volume: Q = HR × SV. At rest, cardiac output is around 5 litres per minute; during maximal exercise it can rise to 20–40 litres per minute in trained athletes.

Stroke volume rises during exercise mainly because of increased venous return, which stretches the ventricle and produces a more forceful contraction (Starling's law). Stroke volume plateaus at around 40–60% of maximal effort, after which further increases in cardiac output come from heart rate alone.

Cardiovascular Responses to Exercise

  • Anticipatory rise — heart rate increases just before exercise begins, driven by adrenaline.
  • Heart rate rises in proportion to intensity to increase cardiac output.
  • Blood redistribution (vascular shunt) directs a greater proportion of cardiac output to the working muscles and away from the gut.
  • Venous return is aided by the skeletal muscle pump and respiratory pump.

Exam tip: When asked to explain how cardiac output increases during exercise, always give the equation Q = HR × SV and then explain what happens to both variables — many answers only mention heart rate.

Long-Term Adaptations

Endurance training produces cardiac hypertrophy — a larger, stronger left ventricle. This raises resting and maximal stroke volume, which in turn lowers resting heart rate (bradycardia) because more blood is pumped per beat. Maximal cardiac output and capillary density also increase, improving oxygen delivery.

Practice Check
1. Define cardiac output and state the equation used to calculate it. +
Cardiac output is the volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute. Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume (Q = HR × SV).
2. Explain how stroke volume increases during exercise. +
Model answer points:
  • Venous return increases during exercise (aided by the muscle and respiratory pumps).
  • This stretches the ventricle walls more.
  • By Starling's law, greater stretch produces a more forceful contraction, ejecting more blood per beat.
3. Describe the role of the SA node in the cardiac cycle. +
Model answer points:
  • The SA node is the heart's natural pacemaker.
  • It generates the electrical impulse that initiates each heartbeat (the heart is myogenic).
  • The impulse spreads across the atria, causing them to contract.
4. Explain why an endurance athlete has a lower resting heart rate than an untrained person. +
Model answer points:
  • Endurance training causes hypertrophy of the left ventricle, increasing stroke volume.
  • A greater stroke volume means more blood is pumped per beat.
  • To maintain the same resting cardiac output, heart rate falls (bradycardia).

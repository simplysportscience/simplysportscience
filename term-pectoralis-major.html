Pectoralis Major

The large chest muscle behind every pushing and throwing action — a prime mover at the shoulder.

📖What it means

The pectoralis major is a large muscle covering the front of the chest. Its main actions at the shoulder are horizontal adduction (bringing the arm across the body), flexion and medial rotation of the arm.

It is heavily recruited in pushing and throwing actions — any movement that drives the arm forward or across the body against resistance. It acts as an agonist in these actions and works with other shoulder muscles.

Because of its size and power, the pectoralis major is central to performance in sports involving pushing, throwing and striking, and is a common focus in movement analysis of the upper body.

🏅Sporting examples

Throwing

The pectoralis major contributes power to an overarm throw or a shot put by driving the arm forward across the body.

Boxing

A boxer's straight punch uses the pectoralis major to drive the arm forward powerfully.

Weight training

A bench press or press-up strongly recruits the pectoralis major to push the arms forward against resistance.

✍️Using the term in context

Name its actions and role in pushing/throwing:

"The pectoralis major flexes, horizontally adducts and medially rotates the arm at the shoulder; it acts as the agonist in pushing and throwing actions such as a bench press or a punch."

Identify it as the prime mover in pushing movements.

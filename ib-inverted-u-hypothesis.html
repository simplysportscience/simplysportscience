Inverted-U Hypothesis

Too little arousal and you're flat; too much and you fall apart. Somewhere in between is your peak. The inverted-U captures that sweet spot — and its limits.

Key Terms

Inverted-U HypothesisThe theory that performance improves with arousal up to an optimal point, then declines.
Optimal ArousalThe level of arousal at which performance peaks for a given task.
Yerkes-Dodson LawThe original principle underpinning the inverted-U, including the effect of task difficulty.
Drive TheoryAn alternative model stating performance increases linearly with arousal for well-learned skills.
Catastrophe TheoryA model where excessive arousal causes a sudden, dramatic drop in performance.

The Basic Relationship

The Inverted-U Hypothesis

The inverted-U hypothesis proposes that as arousal increases, performance improves — but only up to an optimal point. Beyond that point, further arousal causes performance to decline. Plotted on a graph, this produces the characteristic inverted-U shape.

Optimal Arousal Varies

Crucially, the optimal level of arousal is not fixed — it depends on the task and the performer (an idea rooted in the Yerkes-Dodson law):

  • Simple/gross skills (e.g. a maximal lift, a rugby tackle) have a higher optimal arousal
  • Complex/fine skills (e.g. a golf putt, an archery shot) have a lower optimal arousal
  • Skilled performers tend to tolerate higher arousal than beginners

Exam tip: Don't just draw the curve — state that the optimal point shifts with task complexity and skill level. That's where higher marks are earned.

Comparison with Other Theories

TheoryArousal–performance relationship
Drive theoryPerformance rises linearly with arousal (dominant response emerges)
Inverted-UPerformance peaks at optimal arousal, then declines
Catastrophe theoryAbove optimal, high cognitive anxiety causes a sudden, dramatic drop

The inverted-U improves on drive theory by explaining why over-arousal harms performance, while catastrophe theory refines it further by describing a sudden collapse rather than a gentle decline.

Practice Check
1. Describe the relationship between arousal and performance according to the inverted-U hypothesis. +
Model answer points:
  • As arousal increases, performance improves up to an optimal point.
  • Beyond the optimal point, further increases in arousal cause performance to decline.
  • This produces an inverted-U shaped curve.
2. Explain how the optimal level of arousal differs between a golf putt and a rugby tackle. +
Model answer points:
  • A golf putt is a fine/complex skill with a lower optimal arousal.
  • A rugby tackle is a gross/simple skill with a higher optimal arousal.
  • Higher arousal would disrupt the fine control of the putt but benefit the powerful tackle.
3. Compare the inverted-U hypothesis with drive theory. +
Model answer points:
  • Drive theory states performance increases linearly with arousal (favouring the dominant response).
  • The inverted-U states performance peaks at an optimal arousal, then declines.
  • The inverted-U better explains why very high arousal can harm performance, which drive theory does not.
4. Explain how catastrophe theory refines the inverted-U hypothesis. +
Model answer points:
  • The inverted-U predicts a gradual decline in performance beyond optimal arousal.
  • Catastrophe theory proposes that with high cognitive anxiety, performance drops suddenly and dramatically once arousal passes the optimal point.
  • Recovery then requires arousal to be substantially reduced.

Sociocultural Issues

What sport and physical education are, the values and undesirable outcomes of sport, participation, and the pathways to excellence.

1Overview 2Presentation 3Deep Dive 4Exam Tips 5Video 6Practice Questions
1

Overview

Sociocultural issues ask how sport and physical activity fit into society. You need to distinguish sport from physical education, explain the values and undesirable outcomes of sport, and explain why people do or do not take part in physical activity. The final part of the topic covers how countries and clubs try to produce elite performers.

These are discussion topics. Examiners expect structured argument, use of examples and a clear judgement, especially when you evaluate.

Key Terms

SportA competitive, highly structured physical activity with complex rules, officials and predetermined boundaries.
Physical educationPlanned, progressive learning of movement, delivered to all pupils as part of the school curriculum.
Sociocultural influencesSocial, cultural and economic factors that shape who takes part in sport and how.
Participation ethicThe belief that taking part in sport is valuable in itself, whatever the result.
Elite performerA performer at the top of the performance pyramid, of national, international or Olympic standard.
Talent identificationThe process of identifying young performers with the potential to reach elite level.

Deep Dive

9.1 The conceptual basis of sport and physical education

Sport and physical education share movement as their basis, but they differ in purpose. Sport is competitive, governed by complex rules and officials, and organised around tactics and strategies. Physical education is planned, progressive learning, delivered to all pupils, that aims to help them learn to move and become more physically competent, using a broad range of activities that are often teacher-led.

Discuss the interrelationship: physical education can introduce pupils to the skills and rules of sport, and sport can become a route into lifelong physical activity. Sport in its competitive form, however, is not the purpose of physical education, and the two are judged by different standards.

9.2 Sport

The characteristics of sport are complex rules, a competitive nature, a highly structured form, predetermined boundaries, numbers, time constraints, equipment and roles, officials, and tactics and strategies.

Sport is valued for sportsmanship and fair play, a participation ethic, physical, mental and social health and well-being, self-realisation, and as a form of social control. Each value can be explained with an example, and each can be questioned: sport can also be used to channel or hide social problems.

Sport can also have undesirable outcomes. For the individual, these include injury, pressure to win, and burnout. For the sport, they include corruption, violence and commercial distortion of its values. Explain each outcome and link it to a specific cause.

9.3 Physical education

Physical education is planned and progressive, so skills build over time. It is part of the school curriculum and is delivered to all pupils, regardless of ability. Its aim is learning to move and becoming more physically competent, across a broad range of activities. It is usually teacher-led.

9.4 Regular participation

Participation is affected by several factors:

  • Economic status: cost of equipment, membership and travel can exclude people with lower incomes.
  • Sociocultural influences: cultural norms and attitudes shape which activities are seen as appropriate for different groups.
  • Family and peers: parents and friends can encourage, model or discourage participation.
  • Facilities: the availability and quality of facilities determine what people can do locally.
  • Age: participation often changes across the life course.
  • Gender: opportunities and expectations can differ between men and women.
  • Disability: access, equipment and adapted provision affect participation.

Evaluate the benefits of regular participation for the individual (physical, mental and social health) and for society (for example reduced pressure on health services, stronger social networks and community cohesion). Weigh each benefit against the conditions that make it possible for different groups.

9.5 Achieving excellence

An elite performer is at the top of the performance pyramid, has reached excellence, is of national, international or Olympic standard, is often professional, and is achieved by few people.

An elitist policy for excellence has benefits, such as medal success, role models and inspiration for others, but also limitations, such as high cost, a focus on a few performers at the expense of grassroots participation, and pressure on young athletes. Evaluate both before reaching a judgement.

The provision needed for excellence includes sufficient funding, high-quality facilities, high-quality coaches, high-level competition, and science support such as medicine, biomechanics, rehabilitation, nutrition and psychology.

Pathways to excellence include specialist schools, clubs, academies, selection procedures and talent identification, college or university programmes, scholarships, (national) governing bodies, and regional and national squads. Explain how each pathway moves a performer from early participation towards elite level.

Practice Questions

Write your answer first, then open the model answer to check it.

1Describe two characteristics of physical education that distinguish it from sport.2 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Physical education is planned, progressive learning delivered to all pupils, whereas sport is competitive.
  • Physical education is teacher-led and aims to build physical competence, while sport is organised around predetermined rules and officials.
2Explain how economic status can affect participation in physical activity.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Costs of equipment, membership and travel can be a barrier for people on lower incomes.
  • This reduces the range of activities that people can take part in.
  • As a result, participation may be lower in the groups that can least afford it.
3Evaluate the benefits and limitations of an elitist policy for excellence in sport.6 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Benefit: successful elite athletes provide role models and can inspire participation.
  • Benefit: investment can increase medal success and raise the profile of the sport.
  • Limitation: the cost is high and may reduce money for grassroots participation.
  • Limitation: a focus on a few performers may put pressure on young athletes.
  • Judgement: the benefits depend on whether the policy also supports grassroots participation.
4Explain the role of talent identification in the pathway to excellence.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Talent identification selects young performers who have the potential to reach elite level.
  • These performers are then placed on pathways such as academies, squads or scholarships.
  • This allows the provision for excellence to be targeted at those most likely to succeed.

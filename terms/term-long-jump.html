Long Jump

Jumping for distance after a sprint run-up.

📖What it means

The long jump has four phases: the approach run, the take-off, the flight and the landing. Speed at take-off is the most important factor in the distance achieved.

The best take-off angle is around 20 to 22 degrees, much lower than the theoretical 45 degrees, because the jumper takes off with a high horizontal speed and cannot generate much vertical velocity. In the air, jumpers use the hang or hitch-kick technique to stay balanced and prepare to land.

The distance is measured from the take-off board to the closest mark in the sand. It is a good example of projectile motion and the conversion of horizontal speed into distance.

🏅Sporting examples

Take-off

The jumper plants the foot flat and drives the free knee upward.

Flight

A hitch-kick keeps the body from rotating forward.

Landing

The feet are extended forward to gain extra distance.

✍️Using the term in context

Link to physics:

"In the long jump, the speed and angle of take-off determine the flight path, and a low take-off angle is used because of the high approach speed."

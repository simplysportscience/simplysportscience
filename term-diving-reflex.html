Diving Reflex

The body's automatic response to the face being submerged, which slows the heart and conserves oxygen.

📖What it means

The diving reflex is a response triggered when the face is immersed in cold water. It slows the heart rate (bradycardia), narrows blood vessels in the limbs and redirects blood towards the heart and brain, conserving oxygen for vital organs.

The reflex is strongest in cold water and when the face is submerged. It is one reason why cold-water immersion can cause dangerous changes in heart rhythm, and it is important for water safety and for understanding breath-hold and apnoea.

🏅Sporting examples

Breath-hold diving

A diver's heart rate slows as the diving reflex conserves oxygen during a long breath hold.

Cold water

Sudden submersion of the face in cold water can trigger the diving reflex strongly.

Safety

Water-safety training explains the diving reflex when teaching cold-water rescue.

✍️Using the term in context

Explain why the diving reflex is important in cold-water safety:

"Submersion of the face in cold water triggers the diving reflex, which slows the heart and narrows the limb vessels, so safety guidance must account for the changes in heart rhythm it can produce."

Link the reflex to the safety risk.

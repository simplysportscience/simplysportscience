EPOC (Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption)

Why you keep breathing hard after stopping — the extra oxygen the body uses to recover and repay its oxygen debt.

📖What it means

EPOC is the elevated oxygen consumption that continues after exercise has stopped, used to restore the body toward its pre-exercise resting state. It is the modern term for what was once called the 'oxygen debt'.

This extra oxygen is used to resynthesise ATP and phosphocreatine stores, remove and convert lactate, replenish oxygen bound to myoglobin, and support the raised metabolism from elevated heart rate, breathing and body temperature.

EPOC has a fast component (rapid restoration of PCr and oxygen stores in the first few minutes) and a slow component (the longer-lasting elevated metabolism). More intense exercise produces a larger and longer EPOC.

🏅Sporting examples

After sprints

Following a set of hard sprints, a runner continues to breathe heavily as EPOC restores PCr stores and clears the by-products of anaerobic work.

HIIT

High-intensity interval training produces a large EPOC, meaning elevated oxygen use and calorie burn continue well after the session ends.

Recovery use

An athlete uses active recovery during EPOC to keep blood flow high and speed the restoration of the body toward rest.

✍️Using the term in context

Explain what the extra oxygen is used for:

"EPOC is the elevated oxygen consumption after exercise, used to resynthesise ATP and phosphocreatine, remove lactate, replenish myoglobin stores and support raised metabolism; more intense exercise produces a larger EPOC."

Note the fast and slow components when a detailed answer is required.

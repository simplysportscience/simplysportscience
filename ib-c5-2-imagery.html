C.5.2 Theme C · Psychological Skills SL & HL

Imagery

How mental rehearsal, when made realistic, can improve skill execution, confidence and arousal control, and how to structure imagery for different purposes.

1Overview 2Presentation 3Deep Dive 4Exam Tips 5Video 6Practice Questions
1

Overview

Imagery is multisensory mental rehearsal of a skill or situation, carried out in the absence of actual physical performance. It uses sight, sound, touch and the feeling of movement, and can be seen through the athlete's own eyes (internal) or as if watching oneself from outside (external). Imagery can help improve skill execution, plan tactics, build confidence and control arousal. Paivio's framework classifies imagery by its function (cognitive or motivational) and its specificity (specific or general). The PETTLEP model describes how to make imagery as close as possible to real performance, which makes it more effective.

By the end of this topic you should be able to

  • Define imagery as multisensory mental rehearsal in the absence of actual perception.
  • Distinguish between internal and external imagery, and when each is most appropriate.
  • Distinguish between cognitive and motivational imagery, and between specific and general imagery, using Paivio's framework.
  • Explain the PETTLEP model and how it makes imagery more effective.
  • Apply imagery to a sporting scenario, classifying its function and specificity.

Deep Dive

What imagery is

Imagery is multisensory: it involves more than a picture in the mind. A good image includes what the athlete sees, hears and feels, including the movement, the timing and the emotion. Imagery is sometimes called mental practice, visualisation or mental simulation. Its value comes from rehearsing a skill or situation without the physical effort, risk or cost of performing it, so it can be used when physical practice is not possible, for example during injury or before a competition.

Internal and external perspective

The perspective chosen affects what is rehearsed. In internal imagery, the athlete sees the performance through their own eyes and experiences the movement from inside, which helps with the feel of the skill and its timing. In external imagery, the athlete watches themselves as if on video, which helps with technique, body position and how the performance looks. Many athletes use both, and choose the perspective that suits the aim of the session.

Internal and external imagery, with an example and the aims each suits best.

Paivio's imagery framework

Paivio's framework helps decide what imagery is for. It classifies imagery by function (cognitive or motivational) and specificity (specific or general):

  • Cognitive specific (CS): rehearsing a specific skill, such as a golf swing sequence or a gymnastics element.
  • Cognitive general (CG): rehearsing strategies, routines and game plans, such as a team's tactics for a set piece.
  • Motivational specific (MS): imagining achieving a specific goal, such as a personal best time or a winning moment.
  • Motivational general-arousal (MG-A): regulating stress, anxiety or activation, for example through calm breathing and relaxed imagery before competition.
  • Motivational general-mastery (MG-M): imagining confidence, coping and control under pressure, for example composed play when the score is close.
Paivio's framework of imagery, showing the five categories with their function and an example for each. Choosing the right category helps the athlete match the imagery to the aim.

Classifying imagery matters because it sets the content of the session. A coach who wants a player to improve a penalty technique would use cognitive specific imagery, whereas a player who is anxious before a penalty would benefit from motivational general-arousal imagery. Using the wrong type can waste time or fail to address the real need.

The PETTLEP model

The PETTLEP model, proposed by Holmes and Collins (2001), gives seven elements for making imagery functionally equivalent to real performance. The more closely imagery reproduces the physical, environmental and emotional conditions of the actual performance, the more effective it is likely to be:

The seven elements of PETTLEP: physical, environment, task, timing, learning, emotion and perspective. The elements are used together to make imagery close to real performance.

For example, a weightlifter using PETTLEP might tense the same muscles as in the lift, imagine the specific gym and the sound of the crowd, match the imagery to the time the lift takes, and update the image as their technique improves. The model supports an athlete returning from injury as well: imagery can rehearse the movement, and PETTLEP makes it closer to real performance as physical work is gradually restored.

How imagery is used in practice

Effective imagery is planned and practised, not used at random. Athletes often start with a relaxed, quiet setting, rehearse a specific skill or routine, and then add detail and emotion. Regular practice makes imagery more vivid and controllable. Imagery is most useful when it is combined with physical practice, because it strengthens the planning and rehearsal of the skill, while physical practice develops the actual movement.

There are limits. Imagery cannot replace physical training, and its effects are usually larger for well-learned skills than for complex new ones. Vividness and controllability vary between athletes, and a poorly structured or unrealistic image may not help. Coaches should check that imagery supports, rather than replaces, the training programme.

Exam Tips

🎯
Define it as multisensory rehearsalUse the phrase "multisensory mental rehearsal in the absence of actual perception", then give one sporting example. Check the IB command terms.
🧩
Classify with both termsName the function (cognitive or motivational) and the specificity (specific or general), for example "motivational general-arousal imagery". This is the core of Paivio questions.
🎥
Choose the perspective with a reasonInternal imagery suits feel and timing; external imagery suits technique and body position. Justify the choice for the athlete in the question.
🔁
Apply PETTLEP to the scenarioFor "how to make imagery effective" questions, name the relevant PETTLEP elements and say how each would be applied to the sport.
⚠️
Common mistakeDon't describe imagery as "just visualising" or as a replacement for physical practice. It is multisensory and works best alongside physical training.

5(HL) Explain how the PETTLEP model could be used to make imagery more effective for a sprinter preparing for a final.4 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Physical: the sprinter tenses and moves the muscles as in the start and drive phase.
  • Environment and timing: they imagine the specific track and crowd, and match the duration of the imagery to the race.
  • Emotion and learning: they include the pressure of the final, and update the imagery as their technique improves.
  • Perspective: they choose internal or external imagery depending on whether they are working on feel or on technique.

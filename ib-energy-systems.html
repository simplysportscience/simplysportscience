Energy Systems

Every muscle contraction is paid for in ATP. Here's how the body rebuilds it — three systems, each with its own speed, fuel and duration.

Key Terms

ATPAdenosine triphosphate — the immediate, usable energy currency for muscle contraction.
ATP-PC SystemResynthesises ATP rapidly using phosphocreatine, without oxygen; dominant for ~8–10 seconds.
Anaerobic GlycolysisBreaks down glucose without oxygen to resynthesise ATP; produces lactate; dominant ~10s–3 min.
Aerobic SystemResynthesises ATP using oxygen; high yield, no fatiguing by-products; dominant beyond ~3 minutes.
Energy ContinuumThe idea that all three systems contribute at once, with the dominant one shifting with intensity and duration.

Why ATP Matters

Muscle contraction is powered by the breakdown of ATP into ADP + Pi, which releases energy. The body stores only enough ATP for a few seconds of activity, so it must continuously resynthesise ATP — and it does so through three energy systems.

The Three Systems

SystemFuelOxygen?DurationBy-product
ATP-PCPhosphocreatineNo~8–10 sNone (alactic)
Anaerobic glycolysisGlucose/glycogenNo~10 s–3 minLactate / H⁺
AerobicCarbs, fats (protein)Yes3 min onwardCO₂ and water

The ATP-PC system is fastest but briefest — ideal for explosive efforts. Anaerobic glycolysis supplies rapid energy for high-intensity efforts but the accumulation of hydrogen ions lowers muscle pH and causes fatigue. The aerobic system yields far more ATP per molecule of fuel and produces no fatiguing by-products, but is slower to supply energy.

The Energy Continuum

No system works in isolation. All three contribute simultaneously; the dominant system depends on the intensity and duration of the activity. A 100 m sprint is dominated by the ATP-PC system, a 400 m by anaerobic glycolysis, and a marathon by the aerobic system.

Exam tip: When asked which system dominates a given activity, justify your answer with both the duration and the intensity — e.g. "a 400 m run lasts ~45–60 seconds at high intensity, so anaerobic glycolysis predominates."

Recovery

After exercise, phosphocreatine stores are replenished within a few minutes, and the body takes in extra oxygen (EPOC — excess post-exercise oxygen consumption) to restore ATP and PC, remove metabolic by-products and return the body to its resting state.

Practice Check
1. Name the three energy systems and state the approximate duration each predominates. +
Model answer points:
  • ATP-PC system — up to around 8–10 seconds.
  • Anaerobic glycolysis — around 10 seconds to 3 minutes.
  • Aerobic system — beyond around 3 minutes.
2. Explain why the ATP-PC system can only fuel very short, explosive efforts. +
Model answer points:
  • It resynthesises ATP using phosphocreatine stored in the muscle.
  • These stores are very limited, lasting only around 8–10 seconds of maximal effort.
  • Once depleted, another system must take over.
3. Identify the by-product of anaerobic glycolysis and explain how it contributes to fatigue. +
Model answer points:
  • Anaerobic glycolysis produces lactate, and hydrogen ions accumulate.
  • The accumulation of hydrogen ions lowers muscle pH (increases acidity).
  • This impairs muscle contraction and enzyme activity, contributing to fatigue.
4. Using the energy continuum, explain which system dominates a marathon and why. +
Model answer points:
  • The aerobic system dominates a marathon.
  • The event is long duration (over two hours) and relatively low-to-moderate intensity.
  • The aerobic system provides a high, sustainable ATP yield using carbohydrate and fat, without fatiguing by-products.

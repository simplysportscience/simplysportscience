Teacher Hub

How the qualification is structured, assessed and graded, and the support available for planning.

1

Qualification overview

This page is based on the Pearson BTEC International Level 3 Qualifications in Sport specification, Issue 3 (October 2026). First teaching is from September 2026, and first certification is from 2028. The qualification replaces the Pearson BTEC International Level 3 in Sport (2020), and Pearson's transition guide sets out the differences between the two.

Every unit is at Level 3 and worth 60 guided learning hours (GLH). The size of a qualification therefore depends on the number of units a student completes. The structures are designed so that a student who starts on a smaller size can progress to a larger one.

3

Assessment

All units are internally assessed and subject to external standards verification. A centre must be approved by Pearson before it assesses. Units are assessed in one of two ways:

  • Pearson Set Assignment Briefs (PSABs) are set by Pearson and marked by the centre. Units 1 to 8 are assessed this way. Pearson releases PSABs annually for each assessment window, and centres must use the PSAB specified for the relevant series.
  • Centre-set assignments are used for units 9 to 24. Centres can set their own assignments or use an Authorised Assignment Brief (AAB) provided by Pearson and approved by Pearson Standards Verifiers.

Resubmissions and retakes

  • A student who has not achieved their expected level of performance may be allowed one resubmission of improved evidence for each assignment, if the Lead Internal Verifier authorises it.
  • An authorised resubmission must be completed within 15 working days of the student receiving the assessment result.
  • Feedback may only clarify where the expected level was not achieved. Students must not be given specific guidance on how to improve the work, or solutions to the tasks.
  • If the pass criteria are still not met after a resubmission, the Lead Internal Verifier may authorise one retake in exceptional circumstances. The retake deadline must fall within the same academic year.
4

Grading

Each unit is graded Distinction (D), Merit (M), Pass (P) or Unclassified (U). Unit grades convert to points, and the points are added up to give the qualification grade.

Awarding rules

  • Students must complete all units in a valid combination and have an outcome (D, M, P or U) for each.
  • Every mandatory unit must be achieved at Pass or above.
  • The total points must reach the threshold for a grade. Students below the Pass threshold are recorded as Unclassified and are not certificated.
  • A student who does not meet the requirements for their size may be awarded a smaller qualification in the suite, if they have achieved a valid combination of units for it.
5

Entry and planning

There are no formal entry requirements, but centres must recruit students who have a reasonable expectation of success. Students are expected to hold qualifications at or equivalent to Level 2, such as five international GCSEs at good grades and/or a BTEC Level 2 qualification. Students studying in English are recommended to have at least CEFR Level B2 or Pearson Global Scale of English 51.

Pearson provides the following support:

  • Delivery guides and sample schemes of work for each mandatory unit and a selection of optional units.
  • Sample delivery plans for every qualification size.
  • A resources guide setting out the minimum resources for each unit.
  • A sample assignment for each Pearson Set Assignment unit, and Authorised Assignment Briefs for the other units.
  • The transition guide from the 2020 qualification.
  • Pearson Progress, a digital system for planning and managing assessment.
  • The Supplementary Information document, which covers preparing for set assignments and reasonable adjustments.

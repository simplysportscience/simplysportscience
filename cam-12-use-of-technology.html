Topic 12 Theme 3 · Sociocultural issues and technology AS Level 8386

The Use of Technology

The technology used in sport, from equipment and clothing to cameras and software, and the advantages and disadvantages it brings to each group.

1Overview 2Presentation 3Deep Dive 4Exam Tips 5Video 6Practice Questions

Overview

Technology is used throughout sport: in equipment, clothing, footwear, surfaces, facilities, cameras and software. Each example can improve performance, safety or the spectator’s experience, but each also brings disadvantages. This topic asks you to explain examples of technology, and to evaluate its advantages and disadvantages for performers, sponsors, the event, and both types of spectator.

Key Terms

Force plateA measuring device that records the forces a performer applies to the ground.
Photo-finishA camera system that records the moment of finishing, used to decide close results.
Motion trackingSoftware that records the movement of players or performers over time.
Compression wearTight-fitting clothing that is claimed to improve circulation and recovery.
All-weather pitchA synthetic playing surface that can be used in all conditions.

Deep Dive

12.1 Examples of technology in sport

Technology in sport includes:

  • Equipment, such as tennis rackets and force plates.
  • Clothing, such as compression wear.
  • Footwear, such as running shoes.
  • Surfaces, such as all-weather pitches.
  • Facilities, such as sport-specific venues and wind tunnels.
  • Cameras, such as photo-finish and video replay.
  • Software, such as biomechanical analysis and motion tracking of players.

For each example, explain how it is used and what it measures or changes, using a sporting example.

12.2 Evaluating technology

Technology has advantages and disadvantages for each group. Evaluate each in turn:

  • The performer, team or coach: objective data and better feedback, but cost, dependence on equipment and access inequality between teams.
  • The sponsor: visibility through branded equipment and technology, but cost and risk if the technology fails.
  • The event, sport or official: fairer decisions through review, but slower play and disputes about how the technology is used.
  • Primary spectators (at the event): better information and replays, but possible disruption and delays.
  • Secondary spectators (remote audience): clearer coverage and analysis, but the risk that the viewing experience is shaped by the technology.

Make a judgement about which group benefits most, and whether the benefits outweigh the disadvantages in the context given.

Practice Questions

Write your answer first, then open the model answer to check it.

1Explain how a force plate can be used in the analysis of a sprint start.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • A force plate records the forces the performer applies to the ground.
  • This data shows the force, its direction and how it changes through the start.
  • It can be used to identify weaknesses and to plan training.
2Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of photo-finish cameras for officials.3 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Advantage: the photo-finish gives an objective decision in close results.
  • Disadvantage: the technology can take time, which delays the official result and can disrupt the event.
3Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of motion tracking software for a coach.4 marksAnswer
Model answer
  • Advantage: objective data on player movement can inform tactics and training.
  • Advantage: data can be used to track improvement over time.
  • Disadvantage: the software is expensive, so it may be available only to well-funded teams.
  • Disadvantage: coaches may rely on data over observation and experience.

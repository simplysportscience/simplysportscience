Atrioventricular Node (AVN)

The heart's relay and timekeeper — it delays the electrical impulse so the chambers pump in the right order.

📖What it means

The atrioventricular node (AVN) is part of the heart's electrical conduction system. It acts as a relay between the atria and the ventricles.

When the impulse from the SA node reaches the AVN, the AVN briefly delays it. This delay is functionally important: it allows the atria to finish contracting and fully empty their blood into the ventricles before the ventricles contract.

From the AVN, the impulse passes down the bundle of His and through the Purkinje fibres, spreading through the ventricle walls and causing them to contract. The AVN's timing role is essential to the coordinated, efficient pumping of the heart.

🏅Sporting examples

Coordinated pumping

The AVN's delay ensures the atria empty into the ventricles before the ventricles contract, making each beat efficient.

Conduction pathway

The impulse travels SA node → AVN → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres, coordinating the heartbeat.

Myogenic control

As part of the conduction system, the AVN helps the heart generate and coordinate its own beat (myogenic).

✍️Using the term in context

State the AVN's role and why its delay matters:

"The atrioventricular node relays the electrical impulse from the atria to the ventricles and briefly delays it, allowing the atria to finish emptying into the ventricles before the ventricles contract."

Place it correctly in the conduction sequence after the SA node.
